Expertise

What I handle, end to end.

A complete view of the HR, industrial-relations and compliance work that keeps a textile unit running smoothly.

01 · Human Resources

HR Operations

Assisting and running the daily operations of the HR function with accuracy and discretion.

  • Compile and update employee records — hard and soft copies
  • Process documentation and personnel reports: staffing, recruitment, training, grievances, performance evaluations
  • Respond to employee requests on HR issues, rules and regulations
  • Provide payroll data — absences, bonus, leave — process payroll and resolve errors
  • Schedule meetings, interviews and HR events; maintain agendas
  • Run orientations and update records of new staff
  • Support all internal and external HR enquiries
  • Stay current with HR trends and best practices
02 · Workplace

Industrial Relations

Keeping the plant a place where welfare, safety and communication are never an afterthought.

  • Provide welfare facilities — canteen, drinking water, first-aid box, rest room, ambulance — and check regularly that they actually reach employees
  • Handle employee grievances and keep communication open so they’re settled quickly
  • Prepare and display notices
  • Liaise with other departments across the plant
  • Coordinate safety compliance with production, quality, safety and security teams, and keep the records
03 · Engagement

Employee Relations

Building communication channels for workmen and a habit of listening to what the floor is saying.

  • Establish and sustain monthly HR meets with workmen
  • Run committee meetings: grievance redressal, transport, canteen, prevention of sexual harassment and works committee
  • Take a proactive pulse-check of the internal and external environment
04 · Growth

Training & Development

A simple, repeatable cycle that links training directly to what each department needs.

  • Identify training needs in discussion with department heads
  • Prepare the training calendar
  • Organise the training programmes
  • Collect feedback from participants
  • Forward the feedback to the HR head

Good to know

HR in a textile plant.

A few plain-language answers about what the role involves in a manufacturing environment.

Beyond hiring and payroll, the role covers worker welfare, grievance handling, statutory records, safety coordination, training and — for certified units — the social-compliance audits that buyers expect.

Standards such as GOTS, GRS and buyer programmes like Inditex check how people are treated, not just how products are made. Good records and real welfare practices protect both workers and business relationships.

Through open communication, a grievance-redressal committee and regular HR meets with workmen — so issues are heard early, recorded properly and settled as quickly as possible.

It starts with a conversation with department heads about real needs, becomes a training calendar, is delivered as organised programmes, and ends with participant feedback that goes to the HR head.
